# Professional Cinematography Standards For Native Commerce Video

Use this reference when the output must feel professionally directed while still native to TikTok/Reels/Shorts.

## Core Standard

The video should feel like a professional photographer or cinematographer planned the light, framing, product readability, depth, and motion, then executed it in a native creator style. It must not feel like a glossy studio ad, CGI render, generic AI video, or careless phone clip.

## Native-Professional Balance

| Requirement | Professional version | Native version |
|---|---|---|
| Light | Directional, motivated, product-shaped highlights, controlled contrast | Practical lamp/window/store light still visible |
| Composition | Product readable, subject placed intentionally, clean edges | Slight handheld imperfection, not over-centered like an ad poster |
| Lens feel | Natural perspective, correct scale, mild depth separation | Smartphone or creator-camera texture where appropriate |
| Motion | Purposeful push-in, pan, tilt, rack focus, hand action | Small handheld drift, no robotic camera path |
| Color | Consistent palette, skin and product color protected | Natural mixed light, not over-graded |
| Product proof | Macro/details, contact shadows, material texture, before/after | Real use moment, not sterile feature display |

## Shot Design Checklist

Each shot must define:

- Camera height: eye level, table level, hand level, over-shoulder, macro, or top-down.
- Lens/framing: wide, medium, close-up, macro, 3/4 hero, POV, or insert.
- Subject hierarchy: what the eye should see first, second, and third.
- Light source: window, practical lamp, soft overhead, store light, phone light, or mixed light.
- Product highlight: edge light, reflection, texture, label, material, mechanism, result, or scale cue.
- Depth plan: foreground layer, action plane, background layer, and depth cue.
- Movement: static, slow push-in, handheld follow, drawer pull, product turn, rack focus, or macro glide.
- Cut reason: why this shot exists in the sales argument.

## Product Photography Rules

- Show the product at a believable scale with a real support surface or hand relationship.
- Use 3/4 product angles for hero readability; use macro only for verified details.
- Protect the silhouette, proportions, material, color, logo/label placement, components, and accessories.
- Use contact shadows, reflections, table edges, hand scale, packaging, or environment anchors to prove size.
- Avoid flat front-only views unless the product face is the selling point.
- Avoid beauty lighting that hides the function or makes the product look like a different material.

## Lighting Recipes

| Scene | Lighting direction |
|---|---|
| Desk/home premium | Warm practical lamp plus soft window fill, visible contact shadows, rich but not orange |
| Kitchen/cleaning | Soft daylight from side, bright working surface, clear before/after texture |
| Beauty | Soft window/ring catchlight, real skin texture, no waxy smoothing |
| Fashion | Full-body or 3/4 body light, fabric texture visible, background separated but natural |
| Retail shelf | Overhead store practicals, shelf depth, product face readable, natural aisle contrast |
| Tech/accessory | Controlled reflections, dark/neutral support surface, highlight edges and ports/buttons |

## Camera Movement Rules

- Use camera motion to reveal value, not to decorate.
- Keep movements simple for AI generation: slow push-in, slight handheld drift, controlled pan, product turn, drawer pull, button press, or macro glide.
- Avoid complex moving hands plus moving camera plus changing product angle in one shot.
- If a product detail matters, hold the camera steady long enough for the viewer to read shape and scale.
- For CTA, avoid frantic zooms; use a confident product hero or creator reaction plus clean overlay space.

## Professional Reject Conditions

Reject or rewrite a shot when:

- The light source is unclear and the product floats.
- The product has no readable edge, scale cue, or contact shadow.
- Skin, fabric, product material, or background looks waxy, plastic, or over-smoothed.
- The camera angle makes the product look like the wrong size or variant.
- The environment is flat and has no foreground/midground/background separation.
- The shot is pretty but does not prove aesthetic value, function, add-on value, emotional value, or a real solved problem.
- The frame feels like a stock ad instead of a native discovery.
